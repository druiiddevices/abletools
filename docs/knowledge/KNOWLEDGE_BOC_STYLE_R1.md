# Hazy analog / BoC-inspired style system R1

## Purpose and boundary

This profile creates **original** assets using broad, non-exclusive traits associated with hazy analog electronic music and imperfect media memory. It is not a song-recreation guide. Do not reproduce a recognizable melody, chord progression, sample, drum pattern, preset, or arrangement from Boards of Canada or any other artist. Do not use artist or track names in asset filenames.

Use the internal style label `HAZY` in files and manifests.

## Emotional and structural target

- nostalgic without becoming sentimental wallpaper
- slightly uncanny while still inviting
- simple material with rich internal color
- imperfect in controlled, repeatable ways
- spacious enough that texture and decay matter
- suggestive of memory, distance, and degraded playback without relying on obvious clichés

## Harmony

- Favor modal ambiguity, suspended function, and progressions that do not over-explain their destination.
- Useful pitch resources include major or minor modes with modal borrowing, added 2nds/6ths/7ths/9ths, open fifths, pedal tones, and chromatic neighbor motion.
- Prioritize voice-leading, inversions, and common-tone anchors over block-position triads.
- Use occasional semitone friction in inner voices, but resolve or contextualize it musically.
- A chord may change color while one top note or bass pedal remains fixed.
- Avoid stacking every chord with extensions; contrast plain and colored sonorities.

## Chord MIDI

- Use mid-register voicings with deliberate gaps and occasional low support notes.
- Let timing breathe: slight seeded offsets between notes may imply imperfect ensemble or tape response.
- Velocity should be shaped by phrase and voicing role rather than randomized independently.
- Create variations by changing inversion, top-note path, rhythmic entry, one color tone, or cadence—not by replacing the whole progression.

## Melody, bells, and leads

- Build short, memorable cells with restrained pitch range.
- Prefer contour and repetition over virtuosity.
- Allow one destabilizing element: a borrowed degree, displaced accent, detuned layer, or ambiguous final note.
- Bells and leads benefit from softened transients, unstable pitch detail, filtered upper harmonics, and asymmetrical delay/reverb placement.

## Bass

- Keep bass comparatively simple and weighty.
- Use roots, fifths, pedals, octave displacement, and sparing scalar connectors.
- Slight envelope or pitch instability can add life, but the low end must remain usable and centered.

## Drums and groove

- Favor dusty, rounded transients over hyper-clean impact.
- Kicks: compact low body, softened click, modest pitch drop, controlled tail.
- Snares/claps: layered noise and body, uneven but purposeful texture, short room or plate character when useful.
- Hats/shakers: bandwidth-limited, velocity-shaped, lightly irregular, with alternating tone or decay.
- Percussion: found or synthetic gestures with recognizable rhythmic function.
- Groove should feel played or transferred, not merely globally randomized. Use small seeded timing offsets by instrument role, stable accents, and occasional late or early secondary hits.
- Preserve a dependable pulse underneath the drift.

## Texture and atmospheric audio

- Combine a tonal or environmental bed with low-level motion.
- Possible techniques: slow filtering, wow/flutter, amplitude wander, filtered noise, resampling character, soft saturation, spectral dulling, gentle pitch drift, reversed fragments, and distant shortwave-like or room-like detail.
- Use degradation in layers. Keep at least one element intelligible so the result does not collapse into undifferentiated noise.
- Beds should loop cleanly when sold as loops. One-way risers, falls, impacts, and builds should retain intentional tails.

## Synthesis and processing

Useful macro dimensions:

- AGE — bandwidth reduction, saturation, noise, or resampling character
- DRIFT — bounded pitch and timing instability
- FOCUS — tonal center versus diffuse texture
- BLOOM — reverb/delay depth and high-frequency decay
- SHADOW — darker layer or filtered parallel path
- MOTION — slow modulation complexity
- DUST — intermittent noise and transient debris
- WIDTH — spatial spread with low-frequency safety

Keep modulation slow enough to perceive as life rather than obvious LFO demonstration unless the asset is intentionally rhythmic.

## Originality check

Before delivery, ask internally:

- Does this stand as an original asset without relying on a known track reference?
- Is any melody or sample recognizably traced?
- Are the imperfections controlled and useful?
- Would the asset still work if labeled simply `HAZY`?

If the answer to the first or last question is no, regenerate.
